The Three Main Goals (CIA Triad) 
 
The CIA Triad – The Foundation of All Cybersecurity 
 
Every strong defence aims to protect three core things. Think of them as the "CIA" of 
your business data: 
1. Confidentiality – Keep customer data private Only authorised people see 
sensitive info. Example: Customer phone numbers, ID copies, or payment 
details stay hidden from hackers. A breach here leads to identity theft, fraud, or 
POPIA violations. 
2. Integrity – Stop data being changed or deleted Ensure information stays 
accurate and untampered. Example: A hacker alters your stock records or 
changes a customer's booking—causing chaos, lost sales, or wrong charges. 
3. Availability – Keep your systems working when you need them Your POS system, 
WhatsApp Business, online bookings, or CCTV must stay online. Example: 
Ransomware locks your files or a DDoS attack crashes your website—customers 
go elsewhere. 
Protect all three, and your business stays safe, trusted, and running smoothly.

Everyday Examples for Retail, Salons, Mechanics, Restaurants 
 
Real-Life Risks – How Cyberattacks Hit Businesses Like Yours 
 
• Retail Shop — Hacker sends fake "supplier invoice" email (phishing). Owner 
clicks, malware steals card details from hundreds of customers. Result: 
Chargebacks, angry clients, POPIA report required. 
• Hair Salon/Beauty — Staff use same weak password for booking app and email. 
Attacker guesses it, changes all appointments to blank, demands ransom. Salon 
loses a week's bookings (R20,000+ lost). 
• Mechanic/Auto Repair — Customer pays via EFT, but criminal intercepts via 
fake "bank update" SMS. Mechanic's business account drained. No backups = 
weeks offline sorting it out. 
• Restaurant/Takeaway — Online menu/order site hacked (weak security). Menu 
prices changed to R1, or orders deleted. Worse: Customer data leaked, leading 
to fraud and bad reviews. 
These aren't "big company" problems. They happen weekly to SA small businesses—
often starting with one click or missed software update.

Top 5 Threats Facing SA Small Businesses in 2026 
 
The Top 5 Cyber Threats Hitting SA Small Businesses Right Now 
 
1. Phishing & Business Email Compromise (BEC) — Fake emails/SMS/WhatsApp 
from "bank" , "supplier" , or "SARS" . Most common entry point—tricks you into 
giving login details or clicking bad links. 
2. Ransomware — Files locked, ransom demanded (often R100,000+). SMEs hit 
hard because many pay to recover quickly. AI makes these attacks smarter and 
faster in 2026. 
3. Weak Passwords & Credential Stuffing — Reusing passwords across sites. 
Hackers use leaked lists to break in. Many small business accounts still use 
"123456" or "password" . 
4. Missed Calls / Vishing / Smishing — Criminals call pretending to be IT support, 
bank, or Microsoft. They trick you into remote access or sharing codes. Rising 
with load-shedding confusion. 
5. POPIA Risks & Data Leaks — Accidental leaks (lost phone, unencrypted email) 
or breaches expose personal info. Fines up to R10 million + reputational damage. 
Regulator getting stricter in 2026. 
These threats exploit human habits and simple tech gaps—not high-tech wizardry.

5 Quick Actions Every Owner Can Take Today 
 
5 Simple Steps You Can Implement This Week 
 
1. Turn on Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) everywhere — Email, banking, POS, 
WhatsApp Business. Use app or SMS code—not just password. 
2. Keep everything updated — Phones, computers, POS software, routers. Auto-
updates catch most vulnerabilities criminals exploit. 
3. Make regular backups — Copy important files (invoices, customer lists, photos) 
to external drive and cloud (Google Drive/OneDrive with password). Test 
restoring once a month. 
4. Use a password manager — Free ones like Bitwarden or LastPass generate 
strong, unique passwords. Stop reusing them. 
5. Train your staff — 10-minute chat: "Don't click suspicious links" , "Verify strange 
calls" , "Report odd emails" . Free resources available online.
