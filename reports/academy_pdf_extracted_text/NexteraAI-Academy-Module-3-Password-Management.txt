Why Passwords Still Matter in 2026 
 
Passwords: Still the Weakest Link – But Easy to Fix in 2026 
 
Even with AI threats and passkeys rising, passwords remain the main way criminals 
break in. Why? 
• Most breaches start here: 22% of data breaches begin with stolen or 
compromised credentials (Verizon 2025 DBIR) – highest of any attack vector. 
Weak, reused, or leaked passwords let hackers access email, banking, POS 
systems, or customer data. 
• Weak/reused = easy entry: Many SA small businesses reuse the same 
password across WhatsApp Business, email, banking, and supplier portals. One 
leak (e.g., from a big breach) exposes everything. 
• Real impact: Credential stuffing (using leaked passwords) hits hard in SA – leads 
to drained accounts, ransomware, POPIA fines (up to R10M), lost trust, and 
business downtime. 
• Stats bite: Billions of credentials leaked globally (Have I Been Pwned tracks 
ongoing breaches like 2026 Figure/Odido incidents). In SA, phishing + weak 
passwords = weekly small business hits. 
Good news: Strong, unique passwords + a manager stop 80%+ of these attacks. No 
need for fancy tech – just smart habits.

Modern Best Practices 
 
2026 Password Rules: Length Beats Complexity Every Time 
 
Forget old rules like "must have uppercase, number, symbol, change every 90 days. " 
NIST's latest guidelines (SP 800-63B Rev. 4, 2025) focus on what actually works: 
• Length over complexity — Aim for 12–16+ characters (minimum 8, but longer = 
stronger). A 16-character passphrase is way harder to crack than an 8-character 
"complex" one. 
• Use passphrases — Easy to remember: 4–5 random words + numbers/symbols 
if needed. Examples: "BlueCoffeeShop2026!Gauteng" or 
"SunnySalonJoziRocks#25" . 
• No forced regular changes — Only change if breached/compromised. Frequent 
changes lead to weaker passwords (people add "1" or "2026"). 
• Unique for every account — Never reuse. One password per site/app. 
• Avoid common patterns — No "Password123" , birthdays, or "admin" . Block 
known bad ones (password managers help). 
These changes make passwords memorable and secure – no more sticky notes!

Use a Password Manager 
 
Password Managers: Your Free Digital Vault – Stop Reusing Now 
 
Trying to remember 20+ unique passwords? Impossible. A password manager 
generates, stores, and autofills strong ones securely. 
Why use one? 
• Never reuse passwords. 
• Auto-creates long, random ones. 
• Checks for breaches (e.g., via Have I Been Pwned integration). 
• Encrypted vault – only you access with master password + MFA. 
Free options great for SA small businesses (2026 top picks): 
• Bitwarden — Best free overall: Unlimited passwords/devices, open-source, 
TOTP support, easy sharing for teams. 
• Proton Pass — Privacy-focused free tier, modern interface, from trusted 
ProtonMail team. 
• NordPass — Free unlimited, strong encryption, good autofill. 
• Others: KeePass (offline, fully free), Zoho Vault (business-friendly free tier). 
• Quick start: Sign up, set strong master passphrase, import old passwords, 
enable auto-fill. Check HaveIBeenPwned.com for your email/business accounts.

Business Actions 
 
Protect Your Business: Enforce These Today 
 
As owner, set rules for staff – one weak password risks everything. 
1. Enforce MFA everywhere — Email (Gmail/Outlook), banking, POS, WhatsApp 
Business. Adds second layer (app/SMS) – blocks most credential attacks. 
2. Set policies in tools — Use Google Workspace/Microsoft 365 admin to require 
strong passwords (12+ chars), block common ones. 
3. Train staff — Quick 10-min session: "No reusing, use manager, report suspicious 
logins. " Share free Have I Been Pwned checks. 
4. Monitor & audit — Check for reused/compromised passwords monthly 
(manager reports). 
5. Limit access — Staff only get needed logins; use shared vaults in managers for 
team accounts. 
These steps cut risk dramatically – affordable and quick.
