What Phishing Is and Why It’s the #1 Threat in SA 
 
Phishing: The #1 Way Hackers Target SA Small Businesses in 2026 
 
Phishing is when criminals trick you (or your staff) into clicking bad links, opening 
attachments, or sharing info like passwords/OTPs via email, SMS, WhatsApp, or calls. 
They pretend to be trusted sources (SARS, FNB, Takealot, your supplier) to steal money, 
data, or lock your systems. 
Why It's #1 in South Africa Right Now: 
• Phishing makes up 45.7–52% of all cyber threats in SA (ESET H2 2025 Threat 
Report) – far higher than the African (32.5%) or global average. 
• Over 40% of South Africans were scammed in 2025, with many losing money 
(Global Anti-Scam Alliance/State of Scams report). 
• SA faces hundreds of phishing attempts per person yearly, often via 
email/SMS spoofing trusted brands. 
• Small businesses are easy targets: One click can drain accounts, expose 
customer data (POPIA fines up to R10M), or shut you down for days/weeks. 
• Common in Gauteng/Johannesburg due to high digital activity – scams spike 
during tax season, holidays, or load-shedding confusion. 
Real talk: Most breaches start with phishing. Stop it, and you block 80%+ of attacks.

Red Flags Checklist 
 
Spot Phishing – Your Red Flags Checklist 
 
Look for these warning signs – if you see 2+, it's likely fake! 
• Urgent or threatening language — "Act now or account closes!" , "Immediate 
payment required!" , "Your refund expires today!" 
• Suspicious sender — From unknown/Gmail address (not official like 
noreply@fnb.co.za), mismatched domain (e.g., fnb-support@fake.com). 
• Bad links — Hover (don't click!) to see real URL – looks off (e.g., fnb.co-za.com, 
tinyurl hiding bad site). 
• Unexpected attachments — Random PDFs/Word docs (e.g., "Invoice.pdf.exe") 
– never open if unasked. 
• Generic greetings — "Dear Customer" instead of your name/business. 
• Poor grammar/spelling — Typos, awkward phrasing (common in rushed 
scams). 
• Requests for OTPs, passwords, card details — Legit banks/SARS NEVER ask 
via email/SMS. 
Quick tip: Always verify independently – call official number from your records/website, 
not the message.

Real SA Examples with Explanations 
 
Real SA Phishing Scams – See Them in Action 
 
1. Fake SARS Refund/Settlement — Email: "SARS Refund Approved – Claim Now!" 
or "Settlement Notification – Action Required!" with link/PDF to fake site. Why 
dangerous: Steals bank details/ID. SARS NEVER emails links or asks for banking 
info (official warning). 
2. Takealot Parcel Notification — SMS/Email: "Your parcel delayed – update 
delivery here" with link. Why dangerous: Leads to fake login stealing card info or 
malware install. 
3. Bank OTP Request (FNB/others) — SMS/Call: "Unusual activity – approve with 
OTP" or "Confirm transaction. " Why dangerous: OTP = approval to transfer 
money. FNB NEVER asks for OTP via unsolicited message. 
4. Fake Invoice/Supplier Bill — Email from "supplier": "Overdue invoice – pay now" 
with attachment/link. Why dangerous: Business Email Compromise – drains 
account after fake payment change. 
These hit small businesses weekly – one approval = big loss.

6 Practical Steps to Stay Safe 
 
6 Easy Steps to Block Phishing Today 
 
1. Hover over links (don't click) – Check real URL matches sender. 
2. Never share OTPs/passwords — No legit request comes unsolicited. 
3. Verify by calling official number — Use your saved contact/website, not the 
message. 
4. Forward suspicious messages — To your bank's fraud line (e.g., FNB 087 575 
9404) or phishing@sars.gov.za. 
5. Use email filters/spam blockers — Enable in Gmail/Outlook; consider tools for 
auto-scan. 
6. Run phishing tests — Free online simulators (e.g., Google/ Microsoft) or staff 
quizzes monthly. 
Bonus: Enable MFA on all accounts – adds huge protection.
